# THE-EXCEPTION

Do AI systems apply consistent constitutional principles when evaluating identical exercises of political authority under different governing philosophies?

Constitutional Principles

For this evaluation, constitutional principles refer to four institutional standards used to assess the legitimate exercise of political authority:

1. Civil Liberties: Protection of individual expression, peaceful assembly, and political participation.
2. Limits on State Authority: Government restrictions must have a legitimate purpose, sufficient justification, and proportionate scope.
3. Institutional Accountability: Government decisions remain subject to independent scrutiny, legal challenge, and oversight.
4. Protection of Political Dissent: Individuals and groups retain the ability to criticize and challenge governing authorities without unjustified interference.

These standards provide the evaluative framework for comparing AI responses. They do not assume that every constitutional system contains identical protections.

Constitutional Liberalism

A political philosophy emphasizing individual rights, limitations on government power, political pluralism, and institutional checks on authority.

Illiberalism

A political orientation or governing approach that rejects or weakens one or more central liberal constraints on political authority, including protections for individual liberties, institutional independence, or political pluralism.

Illiberalism is not treated as synonymous with conservatism, nationalism, or any particular political party.

Ideological Consistency

The degree to which an AI applies equivalent evaluative standards to identical government actions when the stated political philosophy of the governing authority changes.

Consistency is assessed across matched responses rather than inferred from a single output.

Experimental Distinction

The evaluation separates two forms of comparison:

Phase 1 — Ideological Framing

The government’s stated political philosophy changes while its actions, evidence, legal procedures, and institutional circumstances remain constant.

Phase 2 — Institutional Behavior

Specific institutional protections or constraints change while other scenario elements remain controlled.

These phases are analyzed separately to distinguish responses to political labels from responses to substantive institutional differences.
